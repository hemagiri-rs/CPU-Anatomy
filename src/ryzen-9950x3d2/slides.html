Inside the Ryzen 9 9950X3D2

AMD's newest desktop processor, released April 2026: 16 Zen 5 cores and 208 MB of cache. It's the first Ryzen with an extra layer of cache stacked under both of its core chiplets. Here it sits in an AM5 socket on a desktop motherboard. We'll open the socket, take the chip apart, and see why the extra cache matters.

01 · Socket

Held down by a steel plate

Desktop chips aren't soldered down. A steel load plate, hinged at the back and locked by a lever at the side, presses the chip's lid evenly onto the socket. A stiffener frame, screwed through the board, takes the force.

Watch: the plate and lever lift away.

02 · AM5

1,718 contacts, no pins

AM5 is a land grid array: the 1,718 springy contacts are in the socket, and the chip's underside has flat pads. The package is 40 × 40 mm. A gold triangle on the corner shows which way it goes in.

Watch: the package lifts out of the socket to show the contact field.

03 · Chiplets

Three dies under the lid

Under the notched heat spreader are three separate dies, built on different processes:

  • Two CCDs (core complex dies): eight Zen 5 cores each, on TSMC's 4 nm process. 70.6 mm² apiece.
  • One I/O die: memory, PCIe, USB and a small GPU, on TSMC 6 nm. Carried over from the Ryzen 7000 series.

The notches in the lid make room for capacitors on top of the package.

04 · I/O die

Everything that isn't a core

The 122 mm² I/O die holds the DDR5 memory controller, 28 lanes of PCIe 5.0 (graphics card, two SSDs and the chipset link), USB, and a 2-compute-unit Radeon GPU for basic display output.

Each CCD talks to it over its own Infinity Fabric link, wired through the package substrate.

Watch: traffic on the two fabric links.

05 · 3D V-Cache

A cache die under each CCD

Each CCD sits on top of a 64 MB SRAM die. On earlier X3D chips the cache was stacked on top of the cores. Since the 9000 series it goes underneath, so the hot cores touch the heat spreader directly. The fabric link now passes down through the cache die.

Watch: the CCDs lift off their cache dies.

06 · One CCD

Eight cores around a shared cache

Zoomed in on one CCD: the eight cores line the two long edges, the L3 cache runs down the middle, and the fabric link sits at one end. Below it, the cache die carries the extra 64 MB.

Block positions are simplified.

07 · Cores

Eight Zen 5 cores

Each core has its own 1 MB L2 cache (the bright strip facing the middle) and runs two threads. With two CCDs, that's 16 cores and 32 threads, from a 4.3 GHz base up to a 5.6 GHz boost.

Watch: each core lights in turn.

08 · L3

96 MB that every core shares

The 32 MB on the CCD and the 64 MB below act as one L3 cache: any of the eight cores can use all 96 MB. The stacked part costs about 4 extra cycles per hit. That small toll pays off whenever a program's data doesn't fit in 32 MB.

09 · Zen 5 core

One core, top to bottom

Instructions enter at the back: predict, fetch, decode. They are dispatched into the out-of-order window, executed on 14 pipes, and their loads go to the caches at the front.

  • Front end
  • Dispatch and the reorder buffer
  • Integer · floating point · memory

10 · Front end

Two decode pipes

The branch predictor makes two taken predictions per cycle, so fetch can follow two paths at once. Behind it sit two 4-wide decode pipes. Decoding alone, one thread gets one pipe, 4 instructions a cycle. With two threads, each gets its own pipe. Most of the time instructions skip decoding and come from the 6K-entry op cache.

11 · Out of order

448 instructions in flight

Up to 8 ops a cycle are renamed and dispatched into the 448-entry reorder buffer. They run as soon as their inputs are ready, in any order, and retire in program order from the head. Floating-point work waits in its own 96-entry queue.

Watch: the buffer fills 8 entries a cycle, then wraps around as the oldest retire.

12 · Execution

14 pipes, 512 bits wide

Six integer ALUs, four address units that send four loads or stores a cycle to memory, and four floating-point pipes (two multiply, two add). On desktop the FP pipes are a full 512 bits wide, so one AVX-512 instruction does 16 single-precision operations at once.

Watch: the pipes that fire each cycle.

13 · Loads

Close caches are fast

The 48 KB L1 data cache answers a load in 4 cycles. The core's 1 MB L2 takes about 14. Past that is the L3, at about 50 cycles on this chip, and then main memory, at about 70 ns: nearly 400 cycles at 5.6 GHz.

So the question is how much of a program's data fits close by.

15 · Both stacked

Why “X3D2”

The 9950X3D stacked cache under only one of its two CCDs. Software then had to keep a game on the right CCD. The X3D2 stacks both, so all 16 cores see 96 MB of L3: 192 MB, plus 16 MB of L2.

It costs power: a 200 W TDP, and AMD recommends a 360 mm liquid cooler.
